# Final Project Part I: Left Further Behind
> How America's struggling students are falling further behind in reading and math, from fourth grade through senior year. 

## Outline
### Project Summary
We often hear that American students lag behind compared to the rest of the developed nations. The truth is stronger than this and hidden in a more granular subsection of the American population. In reading, America's strongest 15-year-olds score higher than their peers in almost every other developed country. America's weakest students, however, sit far below them, and the distance between the two groups is one of the widest in any developed nation. The United States doesn't have one education story. It has two, but the average hides them both. 

Prior to entering graduate school, I was a public school teacher, and I was able to see that gap up close. For four years, I taught math at Springfield High School in Springfield, Illinois, where only about 27% of students were considered "proficient" and more than 40% came from low-income homes. Every fall, students arrived in my ninth-grade classes far below grade level. Some were so far behind that it seemed almost impossible for them to keep up, let alone catch up. By the time they reached me, the gap already had years of momentum built up. 

This project asks how and when students get that far behind. Using the Nation's Report Card, it follows the gap through school: from fourth grade, to eighth grade, to senior year. Along the way, the top students hold steady while the bottom falls further behind. The story ends with the students more likely to leave school and a positive projection on those who are able to persevere. 


### Project Structure 
1. The Hook: decades of progress (educational growth), undone.
2. Exposition: 10th percentile vs 90th percentile growth
3. 4th Grade: small declines in the 10th percentile
4. 8th Grade: larger declines in the 10th percentile
5. 12th Grade: persistent declines in 10th percentile
6. Dropout Rate: 12th grade decline is smaller than 8th grade decline, but likely because those struggling the most drop out
7. "Retention" Rate: more students are persevering through school

### One - Sentence Summary
Since 2013, the strongest students in reading and math have held steady while the weakest have fallen further and further behind, and the gap between them has grown in every grade from fourth grade through senior year. 

### User Story
As a teacher, I want to see which students are falling furthest behind in reading and math and when the gap appears, so that I can recognize when a child needs help early. 

### Call to Action
I can do this by asking school administrators how they screen for reading and math difficulties and what support they offer students who are below grade level.

## The data

My main source is the Nation's Report Card (NAEP) from the National Center for Education Statistics. From the NAEP Data Explorer, I downloaded national percentile scores and achievement levels in reading and math for grades 4, 8, and 12, plus the long-term trend results for ages 9, 13, and 17. I measure the gap as the 90th percentile score minus the 10th, and compare how it changes within each grade, since each grade and test uses its own scale. 

For the dropout section, I use NCES Digest of Education Statistics tables: dropout rates by ninth-grade math score from the High School Longitudinal Study of 2009 and the national status dropout rate. I present these as context, not as proof that low scores cause students to leave school. 

| Name | URL | Description |
|------|-----|-------------|
| NAEP Data Explorer (main assessment) | https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/ndecore/xplore/NDE | National reading and math scores for grades 4, 8, and 12 at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles, plus achievement levels, 2013 - 2024 |
| NAEP Long Term Trend | https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/ltt/ | National reading and math average and percentile scores for ages 9, 13, and 17, 1971 - 2025 |
| NCES Digest Tables 219.62a-c | https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d16/tables/dt16_219.62a.asp | High School Longitudinal Study of 2009: share of 2009 ninth graders who dropped out by 2012, by ninth-grade math score, and the reasons dropout students gave for leaving |
| NCES Digest Table 219.70 | https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/2024menu_tables.asp | Status dropout rate: percentage of 16- to 24-year-olds not in school and without a high school credential, 1960 - 2022 |

## Method and medium
I plan to build my final project as an interactive scrolling story in Shorthand, with data visualizations created in Tableau, published to Tableau Public, and embedded in the story. The scrolling format suits a story that moves through the students' paths through school, and Tableau will let readers hover over the charts to see exact scores for each year and percentile.
