# Final Project Part I: Left Further Behind
> How America's struggling students are falling further behind in reading and math, from fourth grade through senior year. 

## Outline
### Project Summary
We often hear that American students lag behind compared to the rest of the developed nations. The trust is stronger than this and hidden in a more granular subsection of the American population. In reading, America's strongest 15-year-olds score higher than their peers in almost every other developed country. America's weakest students, however, sit far below them, and the distance between the two groups is one of the widest in any developed nation. The United States doesn't have one education story. It has two, but the average hides them both. 

Prior to entering graduate school, I was a public school teacher, and I was able to see that gap up close. For four years, I taught math at Springfield High School in Springfield, Illinois, where only about 27% of students were considered "proficient" and more than 40% came from low-income homes. Every fall, students arrived in my ninth-grade classes far below grade level. Some were so far behind that it seemed almost impossible for them to keep up, let alone catch up. By the time they reached me, the gap already had years of momentum built up. 

This project asks how and when students get that far behind. Using the Nation's Report Card, it follows the gap through school: from fourth grade, to eighth grade, to senior year. Along the way, the top students hold steady while the bottom falls further behind. The story ends with the students more likely to leave school and a positive projection on those who are able to persevere. 


### Project Structure 
1. The Hook: decades of progress (educational growth), undone.
2. Exposition: 10th percentile vs 90th percentile growth
3. 4th Grade: small declines in the 10th percentile
4. 8th Grade: larger declines in the 10th percentile
5. 12th Grade: persistent declines in 10th percentile
6. Dropout Rate: 12th grade decline is smaller than 8th grade decline, but likely because those struggling the most drop out
7. "Retention" Rate: more students are persevering through school

### One - Sentence Summary
Since 2013, the strongest students in reading and math have held steady while the weakest have fallen further and further behind, and the gap between them has grown in every grade from fourth grade through senior year. 

### User Story
As a teacher, I want to see which students are falling furthest behind in reading and math and when the gap appears, so that I can recognize when a child needs help early. 

### Call to Action
I can do this by asking school administrators how they screen for reading and math difficulties and what support they offer students who are below grade level.
